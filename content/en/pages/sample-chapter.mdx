A loan is the period during which a book is out of the library and someone
else can't have it. Everything the lending system does is in service of
knowing, at any moment, where a given copy is and when it comes back.

<Definition term="Copy">
  A single physical book on a shelf. A library holding six copies of the same
  title has six copies and one **catalogue record** — the distinction matters
  the moment two people want the same title at once.
</Definition>

## Borrowing

The exchange is short. A member presents a copy, the system checks whether
they're allowed it, and the loan starts.

<SequenceDiagram
  caption="Borrowing a copy"
  alt="Four participants: Member, Desk terminal, Loan service and Catalogue. The member presents a copy to the desk terminal. The terminal asks the loan service to start a loan, passing the member number and the copy barcode. The loan service asks the catalogue whether that copy is available and gets confirmation. The loan service records the loan with a due date and returns it to the terminal, which prints a receipt for the member."
  chart={`sequenceDiagram
    participant M as Member
    participant D as Desk terminal
    participant L as Loan service
    participant C as Catalogue
    M->>D: Present copy
    D->>L: Start loan (member, barcode)
    L->>C: Is this copy available?
    C-->>L: Available
    L-->>D: Loan created, due 14 days
    D-->>M: Receipt`}
/>

Note that the catalogue is asked, rather than told. The loan service doesn't
assume a copy is on the shelf just because nothing says otherwise — a copy can
be missing, withdrawn, or already out.

<Tip>
  When you're modelling something like this, write down which component is the
  authority for each fact. Half of all bugs here come from two services both
  believing they own the answer.
</Tip>

<Remember>
  The due date belongs to the loan, not the copy. Two people borrowing the same
  title on the same day can have different due dates, and a system that stores
  the date on the copy will get this wrong the first time it happens.
</Remember>

<CommonMistake
  tempting="It reads as generous, and it's what a member would expect if they renewed early."
  consequence="A member who renews on day one of a 14-day loan gets 27 days. Do it three times and a book is out for three months without ever being overdue."
  instead="Renew from the date of the request. If you want to reward renewing early, extend the renewal limit instead — it's easier to reason about."
>
  Renewing from the original due date rather than from today.
</CommonMistake>

<BestPractice
  reason="The queue is the only reason a reservation means anything. A renewal that jumps it makes reservations advisory, and members stop trusting them."
  when="Whenever a title has an active reservation, regardless of member category."
>
  Block renewals on reserved titles, including for staff.
</BestPractice>

```ts
// Renewal dates run from today, not from the existing due date.
export function renew(loan: Loan, today: Date): Loan {
  if (loan.reservations > 0) throw new ReservedError(loan.id);
  if (loan.renewalsLeft === 0) throw new RenewalLimitError(loan.id);

  return {
    ...loan,
    due: addDays(today, loanPeriodFor(loan.memberCategory)),
    renewalsLeft: loan.renewalsLeft - 1,
  };
}
```

<DidYouKnow>
  Fixed loan periods are a relatively modern convention. Nineteenth-century
  subscription libraries often lent indefinitely and simply charged an annual
  fee, which is why their catalogues are full of titles nobody ever returned.
</DidYouKnow>

<InterviewTip>
  "Where would you store the due date?" is a decent interview question because
  the wrong answer — on the copy — sounds tidier than the right one.
</InterviewTip>

<Warning>
  Overdue charges accumulate. Any system that calculates them on read rather
  than on a schedule will show different totals depending on when you look, and
  reconciling that afterwards is unpleasant.
</Warning>

<Summary tradeoff="Longer loan periods keep members happy and keep books off the shelf. Every policy here is that trade-off with different numbers.">
  - A loan is a period, not a property of a book. - The due date comes from the
  member's category. - Renewals run from the request, and reservations outrank
  them. - Refusals need a reason the desk can read aloud.
</Summary>
